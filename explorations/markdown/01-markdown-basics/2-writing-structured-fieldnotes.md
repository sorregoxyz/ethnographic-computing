# Writing Structured Fieldnotes

## Exploring Structured Fieldnotes

[Fieldnotes](https://williamwolff.org/wp-content/uploads/2016/01/emerson-fieldnotes-2011.pdf) are among the fundamental materials of ethnographic research. They record encounters, observations, conversations, impressions, questions, descriptions, and reflections emerging from fieldwork.

But fieldnotes are not simply records of what happened. Their organization shapes what we notice, remember, consider relevant, and how we return to our observations.

In this exploration, we experiment with Markdown as a format for writing and organizing ethnographic fieldnotes.

The aim is not to establish a universal template. Instead, we investigate how different document structures can support different forms of ethnographic attention and documentation.

## Why structure fieldnotes?

A fieldnote can be a continuous narrative, a chronological record, an inventory, a collection of fragments, or a combination of these.

Markdown lets us bring different forms of writing together in a single document. We can combine:

- Narrative descriptions and observations.
- Dates, times, and locations.
- Lists and inventories.
- Direct quotations and conversations.
- Questions and preliminary interpretations.
- Reflexive comments.
- Links to photographs, audio recordings, maps, and other materials.
- Self-made metadata.

These elements can be organized through headings, paragraphs, lists, quotations, tables, and links.

Structure can make fieldnotes easier to navigate, revisit, compare, and connect. It can also make visible the different kinds of information that coexist within ethnographic documentation.

## Three ways of structuring fieldnotes

The following examples explore different possibilities. They are not mutually exclusive: a single fieldnote can combine all three.

### 1. Narrative fieldnotes

Narrative fieldnotes prioritize description and continuity. They follow an encounter, an activity, or a sequence of events through writing.

This format is useful when the unfolding of an experience matters: what happened, how things developed, who was present, and how the situation changed.

### 2. Observational fieldnotes

Observational fieldnotes organize observations into distinct elements. They might include inventories, descriptions of objects, lists of actors, environmental conditions, or repeated measurements.

This format is useful when you want to document particular features of a situation and potentially compare them across different observations.

### 3. Reflexive fieldnotes

Reflexive fieldnotes make room for the researcher's own position, questions, assumptions, uncertainties, and responses.

They distinguish, without necessarily separating completely, what was observed from how it was experienced or interpreted.

This format is useful for documenting the development of ethnographic thinking throughout fieldwork.

## Markdown fieldnotes and multimodality

Markdown fieldnotes combine writing with other forms of documentation: photographs, sketches, diagrams, maps, audio, video, or links to digital materials.

Markdown can provide the textual structure that connects these different materials, even when the actual media files are stored elsewhere.

This format is particularly useful when ethnographic documentation extends beyond written descriptions.

## Structuring without standardizing

A structured fieldnote does not need to follow the same format every time.

Some observations may require extensive narrative descriptions. Others may be better documented through inventories, measurements, diagrams, or fragments.

You might develop a recurring structure for a particular research project, while allowing individual entries to depart from it whenever necessary.

The challenge is to balance consistency and openness: enough structure to make materials navigable and comparable, but enough flexibility to accommodate the unexpected.

## Further possibilities

Once fieldnotes are written as Markdown documents, they can become part of larger collections of interconnected research materials.

Individual entries can be linked to one another, organized into folders, searched, versioned, and eventually connected to datasets, visualizations, and other computational instruments.

This opens up possibilities for treating fieldnotes not only as written accounts of fieldwork, but also as components of a broader research infrastructure.
